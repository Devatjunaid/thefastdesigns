About this paper size chart

ISO 216 defines the A and B series. A0 has an area of one square metre, and every size has an aspect ratio of 1:√2 (about 1:1.414), so cutting a sheet in half along its long side gives the next size with the same proportions: two A4 sheets make an A3. B sizes sit between A sizes, and C sizes (ISO 269) are envelopes designed to fit A-series paper.

North America uses inch-based sizes instead. US Letter (8.5 × 11 in, 216 × 279 mm) is slightly wider and shorter than A4 (210 × 297 mm), so documents laid out for one can lose margins when printed on the other.

Worked example

A4 is 210 × 297 mm, which is 2480 × 3508 pixels at 300 DPI and 794 × 1123 pixels at 96 DPI. US Letter at 300 DPI is 2550 × 3300 pixels.

Tips and best practices

  • Design for A4 and US Letter at the same time by keeping content within 210 × 279 mm with generous margins.
  • Add 3 mm (0.125 in) bleed on each side when artwork runs to the edge of the page.
  • Use 300 DPI pixel sizes for print and 96 or 150 DPI for screen-only PDFs to keep file sizes down.
  • Scaling between A sizes is always 141% (up) or 71% (down) because of the √2 ratio.
  • DL envelopes fit A4 folded in three, the standard for business letters in Europe.

Frequently asked questions

What is A4 in pixels?

2480 × 3508 px at 300 DPI, 1240 × 1754 px at 150 DPI and 794 × 1123 px at 96 DPI.

Is A4 bigger than Letter?

A4 is narrower (210 vs 216 mm) but taller (297 vs 279 mm) than US Letter.
