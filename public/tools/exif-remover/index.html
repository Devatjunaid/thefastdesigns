About this exif viewer & metadata remover

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) data is written by cameras and phones into JPEG files. It can include the camera model, lens, exposure settings, the date and time, editing software, and, if location services were on, the GPS coordinates where the photo was taken. Some sites strip this on upload (most big social networks do), but email attachments, messaging apps that send “original quality”, cloud share links and many forums keep it.

For JPEGs, this tool removes the metadata segments (EXIF, XMP, IPTC and comments) without re-compressing, so image quality is identical. The color profile segment (ICC) is kept so colors stay accurate. PNG text and eXIf chunks are removed the same way.

Tips and best practices

  • Check photos of your home, children or valuables for GPS data before sending them to strangers.
  • Turn off location tagging in your phone camera settings if you never need it.
  • Keep the original files with metadata in your own archive; dates and camera settings are useful for organising.
  • Screenshots usually contain little metadata, but edited images may carry software and author fields.
  • If a photo appears rotated after cleaning in other tools, it lost its orientation flag; this tool re-encodes such photos upright automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Does removing EXIF reduce image quality?

No, not for JPEGs and PNGs here: only metadata blocks are removed and the image data is copied unchanged (except photos with a rotation flag, which are re-encoded upright at high quality).

Do social media sites remove EXIF data?

Most large platforms strip GPS data from public images, but email, many messaging apps, file-sharing links and some forums do not.
