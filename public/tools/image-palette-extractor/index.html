Image Palette Extractor

Drop in an image and instantly get its dominant color palette. Adjust how many colors you want and copy them as HEX values or CSS variables.

100% freeNo sign-upWorks offlineRuns in your browser


How to use the Image Palette Extractor
  1. Upload, drop or paste an image.
  2. The tool samples the pixels and groups similar colors.
  3. Use the slider to choose between 3 and 12 colors.
  4. Click swatches to copy HEX codes or copy all as CSS.


About this image palette extractor
Extracting a palette from a photograph is a classic designer trick for creating harmonious color schemes. Nature photos, paintings and product shots already contain balanced colors — this tool finds the most common ones and filters out near-duplicates so you get a useful, varied palette.
Colors are ordered from most to least dominant. The algorithm runs locally on a downscaled copy of your image, so it is instant even for large photos.


Tips and best practices
  • Crop to the area you care about before extracting; large backgrounds like sky or white walls dominate the result.
  • Pull 8–10 colors, then keep the 4–5 that work together, rather than using all of them.
  • The most dominant color is usually a neutral; the less frequent colors are often the best accents.
  • Paintings and film stills give rich palettes because their colors were chosen deliberately.
  • Check extracted colors for text contrast before using them in an interface.

Frequently asked questions
How are dominant colors calculated?
Pixels are grouped into color buckets, sorted by how often they appear, and similar colors are merged so the palette stays distinct.
Can I extract colors from a logo?
Yes. Logos with flat colors give very precise results. Transparent areas are ignored.
