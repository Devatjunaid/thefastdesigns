How to use the Color Contrast Checker

  1. Enter your text (foreground) color.
  2. Enter your background color.
  3. Read the contrast ratio and the AA/AAA pass or fail badges.
  4. If it fails, click “Fix text for AA” to get the nearest accessible color.

About this color contrast checker

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1) require a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (24px, or 18.66px bold) to meet level AA. Level AAA raises this to 7:1 and 4.5:1. UI components such as input borders and icons need at least 3:1.

Good contrast helps people with low vision, color blindness and anyone reading on a phone in bright sunlight. It is also a legal requirement for many public and commercial websites.

Worked example

Grey #999999 text on white scores 2.85:1 and fails AA. Click “Fix text for AA” and the checker darkens it to #757575, which reaches about 4.6:1 and passes for normal text.

Tips and best practices

  • Large text in WCAG means at least 24px regular or about 18.66px (14pt) bold; anything smaller must meet the normal-text ratio.
  • Placeholder text in inputs is often too light. It must still reach 4.5:1 if it carries information.
  • Check text on images at the lightest point behind the text, or add a dark overlay to guarantee contrast.
  • Disabled controls are exempt from contrast requirements, but focus indicators and input borders need at least 3:1.
  • Pure black on pure white (21:1) can feel harsh on screens; an off-black like #1a1a1a still gives over 17:1.

Frequently asked questions

What contrast ratio is required for WCAG AA?

4.5:1 for normal-size text and 3:1 for large text and user-interface components.

How is the contrast ratio calculated?

It uses the relative luminance of both colors: (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the lighter color. Ratios range from 1:1 to 21:1.

Does the checker support transparent colors?

Use solid colors for accurate results. If your text is semi-transparent, enter the blended color you see on screen.
